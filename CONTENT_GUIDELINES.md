# ZEITSTRAHL – Content Guidelines

**Version:** 1.0  
**Purpose:** Editorial standards for selecting, researching, describing and connecting historical content.

## 1. Editorial mission

Zeitstrahl explores history through relationships between historical events and developments.

The guiding principle is:

**"Nichts geschieht für sich allein."**

Historical events should not be presented merely as chronological facts. The objective is to explain how political, scientific, technological, societal, economic, cultural and environmental developments interact.

### Geographical scope – Thematically selected global history

Zeitstrahl adopts a global historical perspective. Events and developments may originate from any geographical region. Their inclusion is determined primarily by historical significance, explanatory value and meaningful connections across disciplines, regions and periods.

The objective is not comprehensive geographical coverage, but a coherent, interconnected understanding of global historical developments. Selection should avoid Eurocentric bias while recognizing that historical developments differ in their geographical reach and significance.

## 2. Selection criteria

Historical events are selected according to:

- **Significance:** Their historical influence, consequences or explanatory importance.
- **Connections:** Their relevance to other historical developments or disciplines.
- **Evidence:** Availability of credible historical sources.

An informal 0–2 assessment for each criterion may help prioritize entries. A total of 5–6 indicates a strong candidate, 3–4 a potential candidate, and 0–2 generally low priority. Insufficient evidence prevents publication regardless of the total score.

The assessment supports editorial judgment and does not replace the existing event-importance field.

Avoid unnecessary duplication and preserve a balanced selection across periods, disciplines, geographical regions and historical perspectives.

## 3. Event descriptions

Each event should explain:

1. What happened?
2. What were the relevant causes or preceding conditions?
3. What significant consequences followed?
4. What relationships exist with other historical developments?

Use clear, accessible and neutral German.

Recommended lengths:

- Short description: approximately 25–45 words.
- Long description: approximately 100–180 words.

These limits are flexible.

Distinguish established facts from historical interpretations. Avoid historical determinism, oversimplified causality, unsupported superlatives and hindsight bias.

## 4. Relationships

Relationships should explain meaningful historical connections, not merely chronological coincidence.

Distinguish between:

- Causal or contributory relationships.
- Shared historical context.
- Reactions, responses and intellectual influences.

Use the existing relationship types and fields defined by Zeitstrahl.

Each relationship must have a meaningful explanatory description and appropriate evidence.

Apply the following rules:

- Never infer causation solely from chronological sequence.
- Avoid presenting a complex development as the result of one cause.
- Use the existing strength and confidence fields consistently.
- Express uncertainty where historical evidence is incomplete.
- Prefer fewer well-supported relationships over speculative networks.
- Events may initially have no relationships if none can be reliably established.

## 5. Sources and evidence

Prefer:

1. Peer-reviewed historical scholarship.
2. Academic books and scholarly reference works.
3. Reputable museums, archives, historical databases and research institutions.
4. Primary sources with adequate contextual interpretation.
5. Established encyclopedias and credible educational resources.

Other sources require individual assessment.

At least one credible source should support the principal claims of a published event.

Complex, controversial or strongly interpretive claims should preferably be supported by two independent credible sources or authoritative scholarship directly addressing the claim.

Relationships require evidence relevant to the connection itself.

Do not fabricate source titles, authors, dates, URLs, quotations or bibliographic details.

AI-generated text is never a historical source.

## 6. Classification and structure

Use the existing Zeitstrahl JSON data model.

Respect the established fields, ID conventions, disciplines, topics, subdisciplines and relationship types.

Do not invent new classifications without explicit approval.

Assign one primary discipline to an event. Add secondary disciplines and topics only where relevant.

Represent historical developments using the existing event model.

Use existing person, institution and source records when appropriate rather than creating duplicates.

## 7. Historical uncertainty

When dates are approximate, descriptions are disputed or historical interpretations remain uncertain, represent these limitations explicitly using the existing supported fields and explanatory text.

Do not invent exact dates, historical facts or causal relationships to fill gaps.

Conflicting interpretations should be represented fairly and proportionately to the available historical evidence.

## 8. Content development with AI

When ChatGPT or another AI system prepares historical content:

1. Follow this editorial standard.
2. Use the current Zeitstrahl data model and controlled vocabularies.
3. Verify historical claims against identifiable sources.
4. Check available information for duplicates.
5. Identify relevant existing events and relationships.
6. Generate only valid, structured JSON.
7. Flag uncertain facts and disputed relationships for human review.
8. Do not fabricate references or unsupported connections.

AI-generated content remains subject to editorial review before publication.

## 9. Integration

New content should be supplied in small, coherent JSON batches.

The import process must validate records and references before modifying existing data.

Do not silently overwrite, delete or modify unrelated records.

Do not change the application's data structure or vocabulary merely to accommodate one batch of content.

## 10. Editorial priorities

Priority should be given to:

- Historically significant developments.
- Events that explain important transformations.
- Connections across historical disciplines.
- Underrepresented regions, actors or perspectives where historically relevant.
- Well-supported relationships that improve understanding.

**Quality and explanatory value take precedence over the number of entries.**
